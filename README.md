# Mexico-Housing-Prices-Analysis

This investigation set out to determine whether size or location has greater explanatory power over house prices in Mexico. Prior to the analysis, it was hypothesised that location would have greater explanatory power than size because houses in cities tend to cost more than those in rural areas. The initial analysis revealed that location explains 56% of price variance, compared with 36% explained by size, suggesting that location has greater explanatory power. However, the standard deviation of 0.36 across state-level correlations raises questions about the consistency of this relationship. Therefore, although the initial evidence tentatively supports the hypothesis, further analysis was conducted.

Feature engineering was performed by creating a new variable, `price_per_m2`, to account for differences in property size. States with five or fewer observations were excluded to improve the reliability of the comparisons. The subsequent analysis of `price_per_m2` across states revealed that urban areas generally have higher housing prices per square metre than rural areas, further supporting the initial hypothesis. An examination of property types also revealed that apartments have higher median prices per square metre than houses, although both categories exhibited considerable price variability, as indicated by their standard deviations.

To investigate these patterns further, `price_per_m2` was analysed jointly by state and property type. The results showed that both houses and apartments in urban areas have considerably higher median prices per square metre than their rural counterparts. Additionally, property counts revealed a higher concentration of observations in urban areas, potentially reflecting greater housing demand. This, alongside differences in land availability, may contribute to higher urban property prices. Overall, the findings provide further evidence that location has greater explanatory power than size in determining housing prices in Mexico, although additional statistical analysis would be required to establish the robustness of this conclusion.
